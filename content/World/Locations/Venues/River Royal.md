## 📜 Overview
Rivièr Royal is a signature fruit wine produced in the vineyard estates surrounding Liora. It is known for its smooth sweetness, layered berry notes, and a subtle spice finish that varies slightly between vintages. Each batch is carefully controlled, with production overseen by House De Rivièr and licensed vineyard partners.

The wine is not mass produced in the common sense. It is curated. Small variations between barrels are expected and studied rather than corrected, giving each release a slightly different character while maintaining a consistent “Rivièr profile.”

## 🎭Reputation
Rivièr Royal is considered a prestige wine across southern trade routes and noble courts. It is often used in diplomatic meetings, marriage negotiations, and high level contracts where symbolism matters as much as taste.

Among merchants, it is seen as a reliable status marker. Among nobles, it is expected rather than admired. Among common folk, it is spoken of as something distant, almost unreal in price and exclusivity.

Its name carries more weight than the drink itself in many circles.

## 🗺️ Location 
Produced in the vineyard belt surrounding Liora, especially along the warm coastal slopes where sea air and sun exposure create stable growing conditions.

Primary aging cellars are located beneath the De Rivièr estate district, with secondary storage near harbor export warehouses for controlled shipping.

## 🏛️ History
Rivièr Royal originates from vineyards that have been held by House De Rivièr for over three elven generations, spanning roughly two thousand years of continuous cultivation and refinement.

The earliest records describe the original vineyards as small coastal plots outside what would later become Liora. Over centuries, the family expanded and stabilized production, slowly turning a local wine into a regulated legacy product tied directly to the identity of the house.

Rather than being reinvented over time, the wine evolved through careful preservation. Each generation refined techniques, but always within strict family tradition, ensuring the core profile of Rivièr Royal remained recognizable across millennia.

Today, the vineyards are considered both agricultural land and living heritage. Changing ownership is not legally impossible, but culturally treated as unthinkable.
