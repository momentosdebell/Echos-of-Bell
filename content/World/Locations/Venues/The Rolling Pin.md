## 📜 Overview
The Rolling Pin is best known for its sourdough bread and slow baked grain loaves, and a reputation for staying open when everything else shuts down.

It is not wealthy or politically important, but it is deeply reliable, which makes it quietly essential to the city’s stability during hard seasons. It is closely associated with support efforts tied to [[The Circle of the Blooming Table]], where its steady production has helped supply field operations during shortages and crises.

> “If the Rolling Pin is still warm, the city is still alive.”

## 🏛️ History
The Rolling Pin was founded in the early years of modern [[Ambervale]], during a period when the city was still recovering from famine cycles and unstable trade flow along the river.

It began as a modest neighborhood bakery run by [[Old Thom]], a former mill worker who understood grain, hunger, and the fragile line between them better than most. From the start, his philosophy was simple and uncompromising:

> “Bread should not stop when everything else does.”

In its earliest years, the bakery served only a small cluster of workers and nearby households. It was not seen as special, only dependable. But in a city where reliability often meant survival, that alone made it quietly essential.

---

During the harsh famine cycles that shaped Ambervale’s recent history, The Rolling Pin became something more than a bakery. When supply chains broke and market stalls emptied, Thom refused to close his doors. Instead, he adapted his production to whatever grain or substitute could be sourced locally, often working through exhaustion to keep bread available even when official distribution systems failed.

It was during these years that the bakery began to intersect with relief operations tied to [[The Circle of the Blooming Table]]. At first, this was informal: a shared understanding between field workers and a man who simply refused to let people go hungry. Over time, it developed into a quiet but reliable logistical connection, where surplus or emergency baked goods were distributed through Circle channels during crisis response efforts.

---

During the major Rivergold plague response, The Rolling Pin’s role expanded again. While not a command center, it became an indirect support node for logistics coordination, providing steady food supply that helped stabilize relief workers and distribution teams. In practice, it functioned as one of the small but critical anchors that allowed wider humanitarian systems to hold under pressure.

---

Today, The Rolling Pin is remembered in Ambervale not as a landmark of power or wealth, but as a symbol of continuity. A place that did not rise with the city’s success, and did not fall during its failures. It simply remained, warm and working, through everything.
