## 🦊The Sixfold

_A pantheon of six gods who walk the world in their true, physical forms — not as distant rulers, but as ordinary creatures and phenomena mortals may glimpse once in a lifetime._

---

### 📖Overview

The Sixfold is a widespread faith holding that six great gods watch over the world from the sky above, each one taking a single, true form that mortals may occasionally witness on earth. The faithful believe the gods descend from the heavens in their true shapes to walk among mortals, testing hospitality and judging hearts, a fox is not a symbol of Redwit, it _is_ Redwit, a firbolg at the _is_ Thalor himself, same goes for all the gods. These forms are simply what the gods truly look like, not disguises or costumes worn to hide their divinity.

There is no central church or unified scripture. Worship is organized around six independent Orders, one per god, bound together only by a handful of shared beliefs, holy days, and the shared destination of the dead: **Everhall**.

### 💡Core Beliefs

Each god has one real shape, and it is exactly what the faithful see. There is no hidden form behind it - this is simply what the god looks like. The gods dwell above but descend to walk or appear on earth in their true forms, moving among mortals not to judge good from evil, but to witness how truly each mortal lives out their nature.

To see one of the Six is to witness the god itself, not a sign or shadow of it such sightings are treated as sacred, unforgettable proof of a life touched by the divine, whether that life is gentle, violent, honest, or cruel. The Six do not favor one way of living over another. A warrior honors Emberwing by fighting without fear, a thief honors Greedmaw by taking with cunning, a healer honors Thalor by seeking without rest. Each mortal is judged only by how fully they embody what they already are.

---

### 🕊️Vows

Each god's vow reflects their nature rather than a universal moral code, there is no single right way to live under the Sixfold.

- **Greedmaw:** Wealth is never truly kept, only held for a time, what is taken will always be taken back.
- **Rainbow:** Love appears and fades on its own terms, never lasting, never fully gone.
- **Thalor:** The mind that stops seeking is already dying, wisdom belongs to those who pursue it without rest.
- **Emberwing:** Death is a fear only for those who do not know its wings, the warrior who has looked upon battle feels no dread, only the quiet certainty of being carried home.
- **Redwit:** Cleverness outlasts strength, the mind survives what the sword cannot.
- **Endrift:** The sea and time move without waiting, indifferent to mortal will.

### 🌍View of the World

**Life.** A single chance to be seen by the divine, every mortal life is a quiet wait for the moment a god might cross their path.

**Death.** Not an ending, but a passage into **Everhall**, a realm holding six great halls, one for each god, where souls are drawn to the hall matching the nature they lived by.

**Magic.** A force earned through devotion or pursuit, depending on the god who grants it, some receive it as a quiet gift through faith and service, while others must chase and refine it through relentless study and will. Both are valid paths, so long as the seeker never grows complacent.

**Nature.** Simply the world as it is, trees are felled, animals are hunted, land is worked, and none of this is seen as disrespect. The gods are found within nature, not above it or forbidding its use; a farmer who fells a tree honors the Six no less than one who leaves it standing.

**Civilization.** Not a threat to the faith, but simply where most of it lives, temples, markets, harbors, and homes all hold shrines to the Six, since the gods walk everywhere, not only in the wild. A merchant counting coin in a busy port is as likely to encounter a god as a hermit in the deepest forest.

**Other faiths.** Regarded with quiet confidence rather than hostility. The Sixfold does not deny other gods may exist elsewhere, but believers hold that their Six are the ones who chose _this_ world, and that is proof enough of where true devotion belongs.

### 👥Organization

The Sixfold has no single high priesthood or ruling temple, each god is served by their own separate Order, much like how a temple to one god rarely oversaw the affairs of another. A worshipper's devotion, and their path within the faith, depends entirely on which of the Six they feel called to serve.

#### Ranks (highest to lowest)

- **Oracle** - A rare, revered figure said to have witnessed their god directly; Oracles are not appointed, only recognized after the fact, and often travel rather than settle.
- **High Priest** - A senior Priest overseeing several shrines or a temple of significant size, often the final authority on disputes of faith within a region.
- **Priest** - A trained clergy member who tends a shrine or temple, leads local rites, and interprets sightings of their god for the community.
- **Devout** - A person devoted to one god, recognized by their community but holding no formal title or duty beyond faith and observance.

| Order                   | God       | Serves                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Order of the Coffer** | Greedmaw  | Merchants, gamblers, thieves, treasure hunters |
| **Order of the Arch**   | Rainbow   | Lovers, poets, matchmakers, weavers            |
| **Order of the Grove**  | Thalor    | Healers, druids, elders, teachers              |
| **Order of the Ember**  | Emberwing | Soldiers, veterans, mourners, grave-keepers    |
| **Order of the Den**    | Redwit    | Artisans, bards, scholars, inventors           |
| **Order of the Tide**   | Endrift   | Sailors, fishers, elders, timekeepers          |

Each Order operates independently, with no single Order holding authority over another. A temple built to honor all Six will often house six separate shrines, each tended by its own Priest, rather than a unified clergy.

#### Clergy
Anyone who has witnessed, or claims to have witnessed, a sign of the Six may petition to join the clergy, no bloodline, race, or background is barred. It draws an unusually wide mix: retired soldiers seeking Emberwing's hall, former thieves turned honest under Greedmaw's watch, healers devoted to Thalor, and wandering artisans who once crossed paths with a fox they swear was Redwit himself. A Priest of Emberwing and a Priest of Rainbow rarely see the world the same way, yet all are respected as having been _chosen_, however briefly, by something greater.

#### Attire
Priests and Oracles wear no single uniform, since each Order dresses to honor its own god rather than the faith as a whole, but all share a distinct silhouette that sets Sixfold clergy apart from other faiths at a glance: layered, weathered garments built for travel rather than ceremony, since a Priest must always be ready to leave at word of a sighting. All cut their robes short at the ankle and wrist, keeping hands and feet free, as if any one of them might need to kneel, run, or reach out at a moment's notice.

Each Order’s attire reflects the raw nature of the god they serve:

- **Order of the Coffer (Greedmaw):** Priests of Greedmaw dress in heavy, dark travel cloaks lined with hidden pockets, pouches, and small, jangling clasps. Their garments are made of durable, practical fabrics meant to blend into crowded markets or dusty trade roads, often adorned with subtle iron chains or weights. They wear sturdy, quiet boots designed for long chases and fast escapes, always carrying the weight of the road easily.
    
- **Order of the Arch (Rainbow):** Priests of Rainbow wear robes of dyed cloth in shifting, iridescent colors that catch the light, blues, purples, and warm ambers that bleed into one another like oil on water. Their fabrics are light and fluid, never weighing them down, often decorated with ribbons or woven threads that flutter with every movement, echoing the sudden arrival of a colorful arc after a storm.
    
- **Order of the Grove (Thalor):** Priests of Thalor dress in rugged, earth-toned tunics and rough-spun mantles stitched with pockets for dried herbs, bark, and small scrolls. Their clothes smell faintly of damp soil and crushed leaves, patched carefully rather than replaced, built for endless walking through forests, healing camps, and quiet roadsides.
    
- **Order of the Ember (Emberwing):** Priests of Emberwing favor scarred leather, boiled hide, and soot-blackened steel plates over dark, sturdy wool. Their garments bear the marks of old sparks and campfires, designed to protect against blade and heat alike. Heavy leather gauntlets and stout, iron-capped boots complete a look that speaks of quiet finality and battlefields long after the smoke has cleared.
    
- **Order of the Den (Redwit):** Priests of Redwit wear asymmetric, clever garments-cloaks pinned with cunning toggles instead of brooches, fitted with deep sleeves for hiding small tools, dice, or notes. They favor rust-reds, tawny browns, and mottled greys that break up their silhouette in a crowd or a treeline, looking more like restless vagabonds or wandering performers than holy figures.
    
- **Order of the Tide (Endrift):** Priests of Endrift dress in sea-worn grey and deep blue wool, layered with heavy salt-stiffened canvas that shrugs off wind and rain. Their clothes are fastened with polished bone buttons or bits of driftwood, and their hems are frayed from wading through surf and tidal mud, always smelling faintly of salt and cold water.

#### Followers
Common followers span every walk of life, soldiers, sailors, merchants, healers, thieves, and farmers alike, since the faith makes no demand to live gently or piously. Most worship the Six not out of fear or moral duty, but from a quiet hope: that one day, however briefly, a god might cross their path and take notice of them. Devotion is personal and practical, less about following strict rites and more about living fully as what you are, in case the Six are watching.

### 📜Sacred Texts

The Sixfold has no single holy book or unified scripture, belief was carried for generations through spoken word, verse, and ritual long before much of it was ever written down. What exists today is a loose collection of poems, sayings, and recorded sightings, respected rather than mandated, often recited by Priests during rites though never treated as absolute law.

- **The Sayings of Six**: a collection of short verses and proverbs, each attributed to wisdom gained from an encounter with one of the gods; commonly quoted in daily life ("As Redwit teaches...").
- **The Witness Accounts**: a growing, informal archive of firsthand sightings recorded by Oracles and ordinary believers alike, some centuries old, others recent; taken seriously but never fully verified.
- **The Hall Verses**: funeral and battle poems recited at death rites, describing the six halls of Everhall and guiding the dying toward acceptance.

Priests are free to recite, interpret, or even dispute these texts as they see fit, devotion to the Six has never required agreement, only sincerity.

### ☀️Holy Days

**Universal**

- **Sixnight**: held once a year at midwinter, when all six halls of Everhall are said to be closest to the mortal world; believers light six small flames, one for each god, and gather for a great feast through the night in hope of a sign.
- **Allsign**: a day when believers share and compare every sighting, rumor, and sign witnessed that year across all Six, celebrated with markets, trade, and communal feasting.

**Individual**

- **Coinfall** _(Greedmaw)_ held at the year's final harvest or trade season, when merchants and farmers count their earnings and leave a portion unspent or unclaimed as offering.
- **Twinrise** _(Rainbow)_ celebrated after the year's first spring rain, when couples exchange small tokens and new unions are blessed beneath the season's first rainbow.
- **Stillwatch** _(Thalor)_ observed on the year's longest night, when the sick and the seeking stay awake in silence, believing wisdom and healing come easiest when the mind is still.
- **Emberfall** _(Emberwing)_ held at the first frost, when the names of the fallen from the past year are read aloud by firelight until the flame burns out.
- **Foxturn** _(Redwit)_ celebrated at the turn of autumn, a night of riddles, trades, and storytelling, where the cleverest tale or trick is said to earn a quiet blessing.
- **Driftnight** _(Endrift)_ held on the year's longest day, when sailors leave their boats unmoored to drift with the current, trusting they'll return safely by nightfall.

### 🏛️Temples & Shrines
**Architecture & Design:** Unlike unified cathedrals, the Sixfold do not share roofs. Every god has its own distinct, isolated sanctuary dedicated solely to its aspect, built where that specific power is felt strongest.
    
**The Sanctuaries:**
- **Greedmaw**, Built like heavy, fortified vaults or sunken stone treasuries tucked away in rocky crags. Inside sits a heavy, iron-banded stone chest set deep into the floor, perpetually locked. Worshipers don't pray for charity here; they slide coins or valuable tokens through narrow slots as silent bargains for luck and clever trades.
- **Rainbow**, Open-air sun pavilions woven with climbing flowering vines, framed by reflective pools or crystal prisms. At the center stands a twin-colored altar of polished white stone and dark obsidian, where people leave ribbons, handwritten vows, or tokens of devotion to mend broken bonds or celebrate deep connections.
- **Thalor**, Quiet, labyrinthine libraries and stone courtyards shaded by ancient, slow-growing trees whose roots twist through the masonry. Seekers sit in absolute silence before a rooted, standing silhouette carved directly from living timber, leaving behind scrolls of solved riddles or written questions for the mind.
- **Emberwing**, Solemn, smoke-stained stone circles perched high on windy ridges or hidden inside quiet, ash-filled barrows. The focal point is a perpetual stone brazier holding a fading, solitary ember that never quite goes out, flanked by heavy weapons or tokens where visitors pay quiet respects to the inevitable end.
- **Redwit**, Bustling, half-open workshops and forge-shrines filled with the clink of metal and clever hidden doors. At their heart is a stone anvil or workbench marked with a fox's paw print in a circle, where artisans and tricksters leave broken tools, freshly forged trinkets, or cunning puzzles as proof of wit and skill.
- **Endrift**, Salt-crusted stone piers jutting out into crashing waves, or half-submerged sea-caves where the tide echoes like a heartbeat. Inside rests a slick, wave-carved boulder housing a slow, drifting band of light that pulses with the water, where travelers leave polished shells or pearls to appease the shifting tides.

### 🌿Rites of Passage

#### Birth 
A Priest presses ash, water, or oil to the newborn's forehead in the shape of the Six's shared sign, while the family speaks the child's name aloud for the first time under open sky. It is believed the first god to glimpse the child that day quietly takes an unspoken interest in their fate, though none will ever know which.
#### Marriage
The couple is wed outdoors, beneath sky alone, never within walls, so the Six might witness the promise directly. A single cup is passed between the couple and their families, and carved tokens are exchanged in place of formal rings. Should rain fall or light break unexpectedly during the vow, it is taken as a sign that one of the Six has chosen to bless the union.
#### Death
The body is laid out with a small token of the god the deceased lived closest to and a coin, two feathers, meant to guide the soul toward the matching hall of Everhall. A Priest speaks the Hall Verses naming the hall they are likely bound for, and mourners share food and drink at the graveside in the deceased's honor.
#### Priesthood Ordination 
A Devout who wishes to become a Priest undergoes an extended vigil at their god's shrine, fasting, praying, and waiting for any sign, however small. Only after this vigil, regardless of outcome, may a High Priest formally anoint them, marking their transition from worshipper to clergy.

### 💀The Afterlife, Everhall

Every soul passes into Everhall, a single realm holding six great halls, drawn to whichever hall matches the nature they lived by, not judged by good or evil, but claimed by resemblance:

- **Emberwing's Hall** for those who died with courage, blade in hand or duty unbroken; an endless battlefield where the fighting never truly ends, and neither does the glory.
- **Greedmaw's Vault** for those who lived by cunning, risk, and reward; an endless hoard beyond counting.
- **Thalor's Grove** for the wise, the healers, the patient seekers; a still, silent forest where knowledge has no end.
- **Rainbow's Arch** for those who loved deeply and were loved in return; an endless arc between skies, forever crossing toward one another.
- **Redwit's Den** for the clever, the artists, the makers; a maze of endless riddles and half-finished works.
- **Endrift's Tide** for sailors, wanderers, and those who lived by time and change; an endless drifting sea beneath shifting lights.

Those who lived entirely outside the faith and refused the Six are granted no hall of their own. Instead, they are bound to Everhall as eternal servants and thralls, forced to haul the hoard, stitch the banners, and tend the fires for masters they never chose to see.
