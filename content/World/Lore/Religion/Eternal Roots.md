## 🌳The Order of the Eternal Roots

A peaceful faith that rejects traditional gods to revere the Eternal Roots, viewing all souls as interconnected leaves on an infinite cosmic lattice.

---
### 📖Overview

The Order of the Eternal Root is an ancient, widespread religion that rejects the worship of personal, anthropomorphic gods. Instead, they revere Yggdrasil as the literal, anatomical structure of the multiverse. The faith focuses on mastering the self, cultivating non-violence, and understanding the endless cycle of death, decay, and rebirth.

### 💡Core Beliefs

- **The Cosmic Lattice:** All planes of existence are different levels of a single, infinite tree, and all living souls are connected through its roots.
- **The Tangle:** All living things, humanoids, beasts, plants, and elementals, are reborn in a continuous cycle dictated by their balance.
- **The Stillness:** The ultimate spiritual goal is to shed the individual ego and achieve absolute harmony, allowing the soul to permanently merge with the Eternal Root.
- **Impermanence:** Change and decay are not evils to be feared, but natural, necessary movements of the cosmic machinery.

---
### 🕊️Vows 

- **The Vow of the Open Hand:** Do not hoard material wealth or emotional attachments; holding tightly to the physical world anchors your soul to lower cycles of decay.
- **The Vow of Harmlessness:** All life is sacred because all life is fundamentally connected. Striking another living being maliciously disrupts your inner balance.
- **The Vow of Listening:** Truth is found not in grand temples or dogmatic laws, but in the silence between breaths and the quiet whispers of the wild.
    
---
### 🌍View of the World

- **Life:** A temporary, physical manifestation of a soul trying to learn its place within the cosmic forest.
- **Death:** A natural shedding of a worn-out form, like an autumn leaf feeding the soil. Cremation and stone vaults are strictly forbidden; the form must decompose instantly to fuel new life.
- **Magic:** The raw, circulating "sap" of the World Tree, meant to be balanced and channeled responsibly, never used to force dominion over nature.
- **Nature:** Nature is the highest physical truth. 
- **Civilization:** is seen as a dangerous illusion where mortals build walls of stone, cutting themselves off from the root and breeding toxic greed.
- **Other Faiths:** Viewed with patient pity. Other religions are seen as merely worshipping powerful entities trapped on the higher branches of the same tree.
    
---
### 👥Organization

#### Ranks 
1. **The Deep-Rooted (Elders):** Ancient spiritual teachers whose bodies have begun physically transforming into plant-like states. They guide through quiet consensus.
2. **The Walkers (Mendicant Priests):** Traveling monks and clerics wandering the Material Plane as the active face of the faith.
3. **The Sprouts (Monks & Clerics):** The core ordained clergy who have passed their trials and taken the three holy vows.
4. **The Seedlings (Initiates):** Novices performing physical labor and learning meditation while shedding past lives.
    
#### Religious Orders
| **Order**                     | **Focus / Aspect**           | **Serves**                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Order of the Ratatosk** | Messengers & Diplomats       | Rangers, scouts, diplomats, and wanderers bridging civilization and the wilds |
| **The Keepers of the Dew**    | Life & Healing               | Clerics, herbalists, gardeners, and healers tending community health          |
| **The Watchers of the Void**  | Deep Meditation & Asceticism | Monks, hermits, and underground ascetics confronting primordial darkness      |
These Orders do not operate independently, but function together as parts of a single living organism. Much like how roots, sap, and leaves all serve the same tree, the Ratatosk spread the teachings outward, the Keepers nourish the followers within, and the Watchers protect the deep spiritual core. They share space, resources, and knowledge seamlessly.

> _Note: Because of the strict Vow of the Open Hand and Vow of Harmlessness, traditional warriors, mercenaries, thieves, criminals, and those who hoard wealth or live by violence find no home in this faith,  such paths directly anchor the soul to greed and decay, rejecting the absolute peace of the Root._
#### Clergy Attire & Armor
Servants of the Order operate under the mantle of **The Heartwood**. They wield no heavy metal weapons or polished steel, favoring armor and garments woven from magically hardened ironwood, organic bark, or living vines.

---
### 📜Sacred Texts

- **The Whispers of Mimir:** A massive, fluid oral tradition of parables, koans, and meditative realizations passed down from master to student. They reject written scriptures to prevent truth from stagnating in ink.
    
---
### ☀️Holy Days 
(The Four Great Turnings)

- **The Rite of the First Sprout (Spring):** A celebration of rebirth where communities welcome newborns and new initiates into the local wild networks.
- **The Zenith of the Canopy (Summer Solstice):** A day of absolute communal silence where followers practice "Solar Meditation" to absorb vital energy from the upper planes.
- **The Night of the Falling Leaf (Autumn):** A solemn festival of impermanence where attachments and regrets are written on dry leaves and cast into a sacred bonfire.
- **The Fast of the Still Core (Winter Solstice):** A three-day spiritual trial in total darkness, turning inward to find the quiet spark of the Everseed.
    
---
### 🏛️Temples & Shrines

Temples, known as **Taproots**, are never built from quarried stone or logged timber; they are grown. They typically consist of colossal, living, hollowed-out trees or deep caverns where massive roots hang down like natural pillars. Shrines are simple open-air clearings marked by a circle of moss-covered stones or a natural spring.

---
### 🌿Rites of Passage

#### Birth (The Binding)
 Newborn infants have their bare feet pressed into the soil of a sacred grove while monks chant, introducing the child's frequency to the earth's mycelial network.
#### Coming of Age (The Softening)
A week of guided, gentle isolation in the forest (_"The Sit"_). Youth practice mindfulness, supported by the community singing softly from a distance, realizing the forest is full of life and they are never alone.
#### Marriage (The Splice) 
A couple's hands are bound with living ivy, and transmutation magic weaves organic wooden rings directly onto their skin as a sign their roots grow together.
#### Death (The Great Return) 
The deceased are wrapped in a hemp shroud, buried with a tree seed over their heart, and left to decompose naturally to fuel new growth.
    
---
### 💀The Afterlife
Death is viewed purely as a biological and spiritual transition. The ego, memory, and individual identity dissolve completely, like an autumn leaf falling to the forest floor, returning raw soul-energy back to the universal lattice. There is no eternal reward or punishment, no divine judgment seat. Instead, the energy that once comprised the individual is quietly drawn back up through the roots of Yggdrasil, filtered through the earth, and reshaped into new life. Those who mastered stillness and let go of worldly attachments during life achieve the ultimate peace: their essence permanently merges with the heartwood of the cosmic tree, becoming an unbreakable part of the infinite forest rather than looping back into the physical cycle.
