## 📜 Overview
The Central Shields, commonly called the **“Coin Stalkers”** by dock workers and merchants as a slure, are a structured security force responsible for protecting trade routes, market zones, and logistical infrastructure across key cities.

They are not a standing army, but a regulated enforcement body tied closely to commerce stability, taxation safety, and transport security.

## 🎯 Purpose
To maintain stable trade flow by preventing theft, sabotage, smuggling, and route disruption.

Their core function is simple: **keep goods moving and markets stable.**

## 🧭 Structure
The Central Shields are organized into tiered divisions based on operational zone:

- **Harbor Shields** - dock and river enforcement
- **Market Shields** - city trade district patrols
- **Route Shields** - caravan and river escort units
- **Audit Shields** - customs inspection and compliance enforcement
- **Rapid Response Units** - crisis deployment teams

Each division reports upward to regional Warden Offices, which coordinate with city councils and trade authorities.

## 🏛️ Operations
- Patrol of markets, docks, and warehouses
- Escorting high value cargo shipments
- Enforcing trade laws and taxation compliance
- Investigating smuggling and fraud networks
- Securing transport routes between cities
- Coordinating with guilds during supply disruptions

In major cities, they are present but not aggressive, functioning more as regulated oversight than military occupation.

## 🌍 Reach
The Central Shields operate primarily within major trade hubs and connected infrastructure zones:

- Key trade roads, bridges, and ferry crossings
- Large markets, warehouses, and customs halls

Their influence decreases sharply outside structured trade corridors, where local militias or informal protection systems take over.

## ⚖️ Philosophy
Order is measured in movement.

The Coin Stalkers believe that instability begins when trade stops. Their laws prioritize flow, consistency, and predictability over strict punishment or moral judgment.

Corruption is treated as economic sabotage, not just crime. However, enforcement is pragmatic rather than ideological.

## 🏛️ History
The Central Shields were formed during a period of repeated trade collapse between inland farming regions and river export hubs.

Early market guilds and transport cooperatives created localized protection groups to prevent theft and route failure. Over time, these groups were standardized into a unified force funded through trade tariffs and municipal agreements.

As cities like [[Rivergold]] expanded into major distribution centers, the Central Shields evolved into a formalized security network responsible for maintaining economic stability across entire regions.

The nickname **“Coin Stalkers”** originated from dockworkers, referring to how the guards seemed to “follow the flow of money” through every transaction, inspection, and shipment route.
